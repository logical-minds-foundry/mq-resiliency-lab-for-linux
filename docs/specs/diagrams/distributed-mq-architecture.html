③ Two sites, two VIPs — the resilience model

Our HA/DR QM exists across TWO data centers with TWO different VIPs. HA floats the VIP within a site; DR moves the QM to the other site's VIP. Everything that connects to it — the app AND the SVC sender channel — must carry a CONNAME list of both VIPs.

Why this matters: HA failover (node→node) keeps the same VIP — MQCNO_RECONNECT rides it. Enforced underneath by DRBD single-primary + Pacemaker: the QM only starts where storage is promoted, so the two sites are never both live.

④ HA vs DR lifecycle — the operational semantics

HA is automated and in-site. DR is a deliberate, human-triggered, heavily-automated single command across sites — and so is failback. The hard invariant: apps can never reach a live QM on both sites at once.

⑤ The MQ object plumbing — message flow

Request/reply across two queue managers: two one-way channel pairs, one xmitq per direction, a remote-queue definition outbound, automatic xmitq resolution on the reply. Follow the numbers.

① app PUTs to SVC.REQUEST (ReplyToQ=APP.REPLY, ReplyToQMgr=QMPCMK) · ② qdef resolves to SVC.REQUEST@QMSVC via xmitq · ③④⑤ sender drains xmitq across WAN to SVC's receiver · lands on SVC.REQUEST · service replies · ⑥ reply routed via xmitq QMPCMK (auto-resolved by name) · ⑦⑧ back through SVC's sender → our receiver → APP.REPLY · app GETs by CorrelId.
The HA/DR test: trigger HA (then DR) on QMPCMK mid-flow — sender link to SVC retries, staged xmitq messages survive & flush after recovery, SVC's sender re-finds us at the new VIP. No loss, no dupes.
